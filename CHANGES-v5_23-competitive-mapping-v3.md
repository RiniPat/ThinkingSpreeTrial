# Competitive Mapping v3 — Research Assistant, Fencing scope, product-specific images

Three changes, all scoped to the Competitive Mapping module. No other module is touched.

## 1. Always-on Research Assistant (specialised per company)

A new `CompanyResearchAssistant` is pinned to the Competitive Mapping screen and is
always bound to the company currently being analysed (the active `mapId`). Unlike the
dashboard's generic Research Copilot (which needs you to pick a run), this one already
knows the company.

- **Grounded answers.** `copilotAnswer()` now accepts a `context` argument. The
  `POST /competitive-maps/:id/copilot` route assembles that context from the run's own
  overview, chosen geography/industry, and the fenced industry landscape, so answers are
  specialised for that company and its problems rather than generic.
- **Suggested deep-dives.** New `GET /competitive-maps/:id/copilot/suggest` returns 5–6
  tappable starter prompts generated by `suggestCopilotPrompts()` from the company's
  overview + landscape. They cover the industry, the company's problems, competitors,
  economics and risks, and refresh as the run advances (e.g. after Fencing).
- The assistant is collapsible to a pill but is present on every stage of the screen.

The dashboard `ResearchCopilotDock` is unchanged but automatically benefits from the same
server-side grounding.

## 2. Fencing scope — geography + industry, with two consulting-grade artifacts

The Fencing stage now takes an explicit **scope**: a geography (India / US / Southeast
Asia / Europe / Middle East / Global, or a custom value) and an industry / application
focus. That scope is threaded into generation and persisted on the run.

Fencing now produces, in addition to the existing quantified landscape:

- **Industry demand map** (`generateIndustryDemandMap`) — a prioritised demand/application
  table (application, products, why used, demand, share, outlook, maturity, grade, price,
  customer type, geographies, leaders, buying criteria, gap, opportunity, source) plus a
  Key Market Snapshot. This is the "industry mapping" view.
- **Competitive landscape** (`generateCompetitiveLandscape`) — competitor selection logic,
  a competitive business canvas (positioning / target / model / strength / weakness / what
  to learn), market observations, top benchmarks, and a "what to build" block.

Both render on the Fencing stage and are written to the "Research for [Company]" Google
Sheet as two new tabs (`writeDemandMapTab`, `writeCompetitiveLandscapeTab`). Prices,
demand and company lists all reflect the chosen geography.

Schema: `competitive_maps` gains `geography`, `industry`, `demand_map`, `competitive_doc`
(migration `019_competitive_mapping_v3.sql`, idempotent).

## 3. Product-specific, non-repeating Scrapling images

Previously every product card showed the same picture — the site's `og:image` (often a
press shot of the CEO holding an award) was applied to all rows. Fixed end-to-end:

- `scraper.ts` now captures each image's alt text (`richImages`) and heavily de-ranks
  people/press shots (CEO, team, award, office, press, etc.), so a hero portrait never
  wins by default.
- `scrapling.ts` evidence carries a rich `gallery` (url + alt) merged across the pages the
  AI asked Scrapling to visit.
- New `productImages.ts` → `assignProductImages()` gives **each product its own** image:
  it matches a product to the gallery image whose alt/filename best overlaps the product
  name, guarantees distinct URLs (no repeats), falls back to a keyword-matched category
  image when the gallery is exhausted, and uses the logo only as a last resort.
- The Breakdown route uses this instead of `images[0]`.

Optional env seam `IMAGE_SEARCH_TEMPLATE` (a URL containing `{q}`) lets you plug a real
image-search provider later; without it, the category fallback uses a keyword-matched
LoremFlickr photo (no API key required).
